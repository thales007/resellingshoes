eBay Strategy

How to Sell Shoes on eBay: The Complete Listing Guide

By Timothy Hales  ·  ResellingShoes.com

eBay is the best platform for reselling shoes. It has the largest buyer base, the strongest search visibility, and a well-established trust system that moves product consistently. But a good pair sitting in a bad listing won't sell — or it'll sell for less than it should. This guide covers exactly how I list shoes on eBay, from the title format I use to the shipping tiers I've tested over hundreds of sales.

Why eBay and not another platform

I list everything on eBay first. The buyer pool is larger than any other resale platform, and shoe buyers search specifically — they know what brand, model, and size they want before they ever open a listing. That specificity works in your favor when your listing is built the same way their search is.

I also cross-list to Poshmark using Vendoo, which adds reach without requiring duplicate work. Women's shoes and anything with a style angle tend to perform better on Poshmark. Sneakers and athletic shoes move better on eBay. But eBay is always where I start.

How to write an eBay title that actually gets found

Your title is the most important part of your listing. eBay's search algorithm matches buyer queries against your title text — every word you include is a potential match, and every word you leave out is a missed search. The format I use every time is: Brand + Model + Style Code + Gender + Size + Key Details + Condition.

The style code is critical — serious buyers often search by the exact code on the tag, especially for athletic shoes. Pull it from the size tag inside the shoe every time.

One thing to avoid: never stuff your title with keywords that don't apply to the shoe. If it's not a Nike, don't put Nike in the title. eBay takes keyword misuse seriously, and buyers who click through expecting one thing and find another will move on without buying.

Fixed price vs. auction — and how to price against sold comps

I always use fixed price, never auction. Here's why: a shoe in a specific brand, model, and size is only relevant to a narrow group of buyers. That buyer might not be searching today — they might search next week or next month. An auction ends after seven days whether the right buyer has shown up or not. Fixed price keeps the listing live until that person finds it.

"A shoe I list today might be found by the right buyer two weeks from now. An auction can't wait for that person. Fixed price can."

Before I set any price, I check sold comps on eBay. Here's the exact process:

How to find sold comps on eBay

1 Search the style code from the size tag, or search brand + model name
2 Filter results to Sold listings — active listings show asking prices, not what buyers actually paid
3 Scan through recent sales to see the price range — note both the high and the low end
4 Count how many have sold vs. how many were listed — this tells you the sell-through rate and how fast the market moves
5 List at the high end of the sold range, with Best Offer enabled — this gives you negotiation room without leaving money on the table

I almost always enable Best Offer. My strategy is to list at the top of the sold comp range, accept or counter offers that come in, and occasionally get a full-price sale from a buyer who doesn't negotiate. The one time I skip Best Offer is when my profit margin is so tight that I can't afford to go lower — if there's no room to move, I set the price and hold it.

Item specifics — what to fill in every time

eBay's item specifics are the fields below your title and description — brand, size, color, material, style, and so on. These drive search traffic in ways your title alone can't. Fill in every field eBay recommends for shoes, and treat these as non-negotiable minimums:

Brand and model. Always. These are the first things buyers filter by.

Size — US, UK, and EU. Include all three when you can find them on the tag. Shoe buyers, especially for performance and specialty brands, often know their size in multiple systems and search accordingly.

Gender. Men's, women's, or unisex. Never leave this blank — it affects which search results your listing appears in.

Condition. If the shoe is new, specify whether it comes with the box or not. If it's used, be specific about every flaw in the condition notes — a small tear in the toe box, heel drag on the outsole, scuff marks on the midsole. Buyers who know exactly what they're getting leave better reviews and file fewer disputes. Buyers who are surprised do the opposite.

Material, heel height, and style. Fill these in when eBay surfaces them. The more fields you complete, the more places your listing shows up in search.

Photos that sell

Poor photos are one of the most common and most fixable mistakes beginner resellers make. A buyer can't pick up the shoe — your photos are the only way they can evaluate what they're buying. Skipping angles or shooting one overhead photo and calling it done will cost you sales.

Every shoe listing should include: both side profiles (right side facing right, left side facing left), front and back shots, an overhead shot of both shoes together, a sole shot showing tread wear clearly, and a photo of the size tag. That last one eliminates the most common question buyers ask before purchasing — and every question a buyer has to ask is a reason they might not buy.

Shipping — how to price it and how to send it

Do not set your postage at the post office counter or through their kiosk. You will pay full retail USPS rates, which can be sixty percent or more above what eBay's discounted rates are. I use eBay's built-in shipping labels for every order — the discount is built in and it keeps everything in one place.

I use flat-rate shipping tiers rather than calculated shipping. Calculated shipping charges fees on the shipping amount itself, which eats into your net. Flat rate is cleaner. Here are the tiers I use:

Package weight Shipping rate Typical items
Under 1 lb $6.99 Lightweight flats, sandals, slip-ons
1–3 lbs $9.99 Most sneakers and athletic shoes
Over 3 lbs $14.99 Heavy boots, work boots, tall boots

These rates consistently cover costs and align with what other sellers charge on the platform. Weigh your shoes with the packaging before you list — guessing on weight is how you end up losing money on shipping.

The mistakes that cost beginners sales

Pricing off active listings instead of sold comps

An active listing is what someone is asking for a shoe — not what buyers are actually paying. Always filter for sold listings before you set a price. Active prices are wishful thinking. Sold prices are market reality.

Not charging enough for shipping

Underestimating shipping weight or being overly optimistic about rates is a fast way to eat into your margin on every single sale. Use flat-rate tiers you've tested, not guesses.

Poor or incomplete photos

One overhead shot doesn't tell a buyer what they need to know. Shoot every angle, including the sole and the size tag, every time.

Keyword stuffing the title

Adding brand names or model numbers that don't apply to your listing might get more clicks, but they'll be the wrong clicks — buyers who leave immediately and don't buy. Your title should describe exactly what's in the box, nothing else.

eBay rewards sellers who list consistently, describe honestly, and price based on real data. Get those three things right and the platform does the rest.
